# Caimeo clarity redesign

## Why this pass exists

The four earlier proposals failed the user’s comparison with the incumbent site: they reduced readability and added navigation, visual and interaction choices before the offer was understood. This pass keeps only one direction and treats five-second comprehension as the primary constraint.

## Product inventory

- Typed intent envelopes
- Deterministic `ALLOW`, `DENY`, or `REQUIRES_APPROVAL` decisions
- Human approval before release
- A deterministic action plane
- Replayable execution evidence
- Trace `t_8f21`
- A governed payment to Microsoft for USD 7,500
- A policy limit of USD 1,000
- CFO and COO approval
- Forseti as the live governance entry point
- Brainstack as shared memory
- Tyche as rehearsal and replay
- Valhalla as a coming-soon delivery room

## Grounded visual directions, ordered by resonance

1. A policy case file whose evidence resolves into one decision.
2. A change-advisory summary that makes blast radius and approvers visible.
3. An incident sequence that reconstructs what happened and why.
4. **A live approval trace whose four states form the page’s spine.** Assigned by `seed:2cacd51612038393`.
5. A release sign-off that separates requested, reviewed, released and verified.
6. A safety case that pairs each claim with explicit evidence.
7. An RFC decision record with the final action visible before its rationale.

## Challenger weighing

- Versailles garden: declined on audience identification and product clarity. Keep its discipline of one legible axis with staged reveals.
- Jacquard brocade: competitive on traceability, weaker on immediate product clarity. Keep the exact line from instruction to outcome.
- Metro tiles: competitive on immediate scanning, weaker on calm reading and mobile hierarchy. Keep flat fields and the absence of chrome.
- Madrid scene magazine: declined on both axes because scatter and angled type repeat the user’s complaint. Keep only its full commitment to one accent.
- Depot destination blind: competitive on state legibility, weaker on audience identification. Keep crisp state changes rather than decorative transitions.
- Film cutting bench: declined on both axes because the editing metaphor has to be decoded. Keep the persistent stop point as a simple stage control.

## Direction contract

### THESIS

One live approval trace explains Caimeo. It refuses the category-default pile of feature cards and the rejected multi-direction chooser.

### OWN-WORLD

Warm white ground `#f3f0e8`, ink `#151719`, signal orange `#ff542e`, flat black rules, Schibsted Grotesk, square data fields, and full-width product plates. No gradients, floating glass, decorative cards, or metaphorical chrome.

### STORY

The visitor understands that Caimeo governs consequential agent actions, believes it because one illustrative payment stays traceable from typed intent through policy and two terminal human approvals to evidence, then chooses to scope one real workflow.

## Quality bar card

- Thesis: the evidence a governed action leaves behind is the argument, not decoration around it.
- Palette: warm white, near-black, signal orange, muted green only for a terminal approved state.
- Materials: flat policy fields, high-contrast rules, real product captures, and explicit state labels.
- First viewport: one offer, one concrete trace, one primary action; no competing modules.
- Honest risk: restraint can feel quiet, so scale and state change must carry the energy without adding clutter.

Raised lines: Versailles contributes one unbroken reading axis; Jacquard keeps each decision connected to the action it changes; Metro contributes flat fields and high contrast; the depot blind contributes crisp state changes. The deliberate phone departure puts the offer before the control because orientation is the higher constraint.

## Argument and proof

1. **Claim:** Caimeo governs agent actions before they reach consequential tools. **Proof:** one real-size payment intent that shows policy, approvers, release and evidence.

3. **Claim:** control expands into rehearsal and memory without hiding the gate. **Proof:** Forseti, Tyche and Brainstack shown in one ordered suite sequence using supplied product assets.
